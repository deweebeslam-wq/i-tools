Split Pipe

Releasedi-Civil

Split a pipe in two at a picked point, distance or percentage

What it does

Split Pipe splits one pipe in two. Pick the pipe, then the split point - OSNAP works, Enter uses the point where the pipe was selected, and Distance / Percent type the location instead. The pick is projected onto the real centreline, straight or curved, so a pick slightly off the pipe still splits exactly on it.

The original pipe is kept, shortened to end at the split point, so its name, handle, labels, property sets and data-shortcut links carry on as Pipe 1. Pipe 2 is new, made from the same family and size, and runs on to the original end in the same direction. It takes over the original's end connection, style, layer, description, reference surface and alignment, rule set, flow settings, profile/section views and plan labels.

The size is found from the original's part data, not its name: a pipe reports a size name generated from its catalogue data ("23 mm Cable") that need not be what the parts list calls the size ("4C أ— 16 mmآ² ..."). Pipe 2 is then checked against the original - size name, material and every dimension - and a split that cannot make it identical is refused rather than built in another size.

A compact dialog confirms the split, with a live preview of both pipes and a marker in the drawing. Enter keeps the existing elevation; E, C, I and S (Alt with them inside a text box) switch between:

- Existing elevation - the split lands on the pipe's own grade; nothing moves vertically. - Cover at each end - the cover at the start and end of each of the two pipes, measured from the chosen surface to the top outside of the pipe, as Civil 3D measures it. - Invert at each end - the invert at the start and end of each of the two pipes. - Split elevation - the height at the split point only, typed as an invert or as a cover there (whichever was typed last counts; the other box shows what it gives); the original start and end stay exactly where they were.

The cover and invert boxes start at the pipe's current values, and any box not typed in follows the split point as it moves. Pipe 2's start follows Pipe 1's end (and the other way round) until it is given a value of its own, so the pipes meet at the split unless a step is set deliberately - for a drop across a structure, where a step the flow would have to climb is warned about.

Typed elevations are inverts. With no structure the two pipes meet exactly at the split but are not connected; tick Create structure to place one from the network's own parts list, its rim on the chosen surface. The geometry and elevation rules are PipeSplitMath in Core, unit-tested.

The split runs in one transaction. Afterwards both pipes are read back from Civil 3D and checked against the plan - ends, continuity, lengths, the curve, the connections - and any mismatch rolls the whole split back, leaving the drawing as it was. Every coordinate and check is written to the log, and to the command line when the dialog's diagnostics box is ticked.

How to open it

Click Split Pipe on the i-CIVIL ribbon tab, or type ICIVILSPLITPIPE at the command line.
